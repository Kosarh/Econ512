01 · Why this law

The public was paying for the water.

Flooding is among the costliest hazards in the United States. Damages run into the billions, and subsidized insurance made floodplain houses cheap.

That discount pulled building onto the floodplain and left the fiscal bill with the government. Risk Rating 2.0 prices the danger at the property.

Does the shock stop at the floodplain, or does it tighten safer ground too?

08 · Where the water ends

Price the risk. Make room on higher ground.

Keep property-level premiums. They pull housing off the most dangerous ground. Pair them with supply where building is safe, or the region ends with fewer houses and a tighter market.
