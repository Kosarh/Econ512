1

Motivation

Why this matters

Flooding is a common and costly natural hazard in the United States. Historically subsidized or weakly risk-based flood insurance lowered the true cost of high-risk housing and encouraged development in exposed areas.

Risk Rating 2.0 (RR2) was introduced in 2023 to align NFIP premiums more closely with property-level flood risk.

Key question: Does a policy targeted at floodplain homes affect only those areas, or does it also spill over into safer areas, and tighter nearby markets?
2

Research Question & Case Study

How does risk-based insurance pricing affect prices and housing stocks both inside and outside the floodplain?
Case study: Allegheny County, PA
Data: CoreLogic parcel-level residential data + FEMA National Flood Hazard Layer
Baseline year: 2022

Policy Implications

From risk pricing to more resilient housing markets

RR2 improves risk pricing and can discourage excessive exposure in high-risk areas.

Spillovers affect safer markets too. Complementary housing-supply and affordability policies may be needed in safer areas.

Place-based climate policy should consider regional spillovers, not only direct treatment effects.

Key Modeling Assumptions & Future Extensions

Baseline Assumption Alternative to Explore Future Research
Perfect substitution. Common rental value based on total housing stock. Imperfect spatial substitution (CES). Examine neighborhood choices and migration patterns.
Common supply elasticity. Same elasticity in both markets. Market-specific supply elasticities. Use developable land and zoning constraints to inform calibration.
Mobility not explicitly modeled. No household relocation decisions. Moving costs and mobility frictions. Calibrate relocation frictions using empirical literature and micro-data.
Next steps: Assess how spatial substitution, supply constraints, and mobility frictions affect housing-market spillovers.
